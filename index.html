Thursday, March 26 · ✅ Confirmed

West Tokyo · Bookshops · Cherry Blossoms

Meiji Shrine at dawn, the world's most beautiful bookshop at dusk, cherry blossoms along the Nakameguro canal, chocolate, macarons, and Memory Lane for dinner.

07:30
⛩️Meiji Jingu Shrine
700m forested path to the shrine. Almost empty at this hour. Forest absorbs all city sound. The most peaceful 30 minutes in Tokyo.
✦ Arrive at sunrise — the forest is ethereal in early light
08:30
🎎Harajuku Takeshita Street
Tokyo's most eccentric street. Vintage fashion, crepe shops, kawaii accessories, impossibly dressed teenagers. Walk the full length.
✦ Kinji Harajuku — vintage branded clothing sold by weight. Marion Crepes for breakfast on the go.
09:30
🏛️Omotesando Hills + Boulevard
Tadao Ando's spiral ramp retail complex — the most beautiful shopping building in Tokyo. Art books on lower levels. Walk the 1km Omotesando boulevard — Comme des Garçons, Issey Miyake, BAPE all here.
✦ Show passport for 10% tax-free at most stores
10:30
🏯Nezu Museum
Private Japanese and Asian art collection in a beautiful garden. Ogata Korin's Irises screen is here. Calm garden café for a quiet stop.
✦ ¥1,300 · Allow 40 min · Worth every yen
12:00
🌸Nakameguro Canal — Cherry Blossom Tunnel
2km canal lined with cherry trees forming a pink tunnel at peak bloom. The most photographed spot in Japan during sakura season. Riverside cafés for lunch — grab a seat outside.
✦ Walk the full length both ways — it looks different from each direction
13:00
✈️Traveller's Factory — Nakameguro
Specialist travel books, maps, leather journals, the Traveller's Notebook system. Right on the canal. The leather starter kit is the perfect trip journal.
✦ Buy the Traveller's Notebook — you'll fill it on this trip
14:00
📚Tsutaya Books Daikanyama
The most beautiful bookshop in the world. Three interconnected wooden buildings in a garden. Art, architecture, travel, photography, vinyl records. Starbucks inside. Open until 02:00.
✦ Go at dusk — the lighting becomes genuinely magical around 16:30
15:30
🍫Minimal — Bean-to-Bar Chocolate
Tokyo's finest single-origin chocolate. Small-batch, extraordinary flavour profiles. Tasting set ¥1,200. 5 min from Tsutaya.
16:30
🧁Pierre Hermé — Aoyama
World's most famous macaron. Ispahan — rose, raspberry, lychee — is the one to order. Also extraordinary millefeuille. 5 min from Omotesando station.
17:00
🌸Shinjuku Gyoen Garden — Golden Hour
65 varieties of cherry tree. Golden hour light streaming through the blossoms. The calmest place in Shinjuku. Alcohol not permitted — peaceful family atmosphere.
✦ ¥500 · Closes 18:30 — arrive by 17:00 for the best light
19:00
🏪Isetan Shinjuku B2 — Depachika
Stop here on the way to dinner. 19:00 means 20–30% markdowns on remaining items. Pick up wagashi, macarons or chocolate for later. The greatest food basement in Japan.
19:30
🍢Omoide Yokocho — Memory Lane
Smoke-filled alleyway of tiny yakitori stalls just outside Shinjuku Station. Cold beer, grilled skewers, crammed next to strangers under paper lanterns. Tokyo at its most unrepeatable.
✦ Arrive early — stalls fill by 20:00. Point to order — no English menus needed.
21:00
🍶Golden Gai — Nightcap
End the night in one or two of the 200 tiny bars. Pick a bar that feels right — there's no wrong choice. Each one is a different world.
✦ Cover charge ¥500–1,000 · Go after 21:00 for the real atmosphere
🛍️ Shopping This Day
Harajuku — vintage clothing (Kinji), kawaii accessories · Omotesando — Comme des Garçons, Issey Miyake, BAPE, Cosme Kitchen beauty (tax-free with passport) · Traveller's Factory — leather travel journals, brass accessories, maps · Tsutaya — art & architecture books, vinyl records · Minimal — single-origin chocolate tasting sets · Pierre Hermé — Ispahan macarons, millefeuille · Isetan B2 — wagashi, premium sake, Japanese chocolate, food gifts

Friday Apr 3 · Nara Day Trip → Osaka Evening
🦌 The plan flipped from a full Osaka day to a Nara day trip — deer at dawn, the great temples, and a climb up Mt Wakakusa — then back to Osaka in the late afternoon.
06:30
🦌 Nara Park at dawn · Kintetsu Limited Express from Osaka-Namba (~40 min). The deer are calmest before 08:30 — mist on the grass, cherry blossoms, almost no crowds. Buy shika-senbei crackers; they bow for them.
08:30
⛩️ Tōdai-ji & the Great Buddha · The largest wooden building on earth, housing the 15-metre bronze Birushana Buddha. Find Kōmokuten, the fierce 1,270-year-old guardian. Squeeze through the "Buddha's nostril" pillar for luck. ¥600.
10:00
🏮 Kasuga Taisha · 3,000 stone and bronze lanterns lining the cedar-forest approach. Founded 768 AD. The deer are divine messengers here. Goshuin available — one of the most beautiful in Japan.
11:30
⛰️ Mt Wakakusa climb · The grassy mountain rising behind the temples. A real climb — steep in places — rewarded with the best panorama in Nara: the whole basin, Tōdai-ji's roof, deer grazing the slopes. ¥150 entry. Allow 60–90 min round trip.
13:30
Lunch + Naramachi wander · The old merchant quarter — narrow Edo lanes, craft shops, kakinoha-zushi (persimmon-leaf sushi). Look for the red "migawari-zaru" monkey charms hanging outside the houses.
15:30
Train back to Osaka · Kintetsu back to Namba. Rest the legs after the climb before the evening.
17:00
🏘️ Shinsekai — late afternoon · Retro pre-war Osaka. Tsutenkaku Tower, kushikatsu counters, old men playing shogi in Jan-Jan Yokocho. Kushikatsu Daruma (original 1929 location) for skewers — never double-dip the sauce.
19:30
🌃 Dōtonbori by night · Ebisu Bridge, the Glico man, neon mirrored in the canal. Dinner — Osaka-style okonomiyaki at Mizuno (since 1945) or takoyaki along the canal.
21:00
Hōzenji Yokochō · The moss-covered stone Buddha in a tiny lantern-lit alley, two minutes off Dōtonbori. The quiet, hidden soul of Osaka to end the day.
Saturday Apr 4 · Hiroshima Day Trip
⚠️ Emotionally significant day. Go with open minds and slow feet. The city has rebuilt into one of the most welcoming in Japan. People there are extraordinarily gracious toward visitors.
07:30
🚅 Nozomi to Hiroshima ⚠️ Book via smart-ex.jp · ~1hr 20min from Shin-Osaka. ¥11,000 each way approx.
09:00

🍜 Ordering Food in Japan

How to navigate Japanese restaurants with zero Japanese.

Types of Restaurants You'll Encounter
🎫 Ticket machine (食券)
Buy your meal from a vending machine at the door. Insert money → press food button → give ticket to staff. Common at ramen and curry shops. Just point at photos if unsure.
📲 QR menu
Scan QR on table → order on your phone. Usually has English. Payment at the end — say "Okaikei onegaishimasu" or press the call button.
🤙 Call button
Most sit-down restaurants have a call button on the table. Press it → staff comes. Point at the menu or say "Kore wo kudasai" (I'll have this).
🏮 Standing bars / Izakaya
Order at the counter, point at items. Pay when you leave or when you order. Omoide Yokocho — just sit down and point at whatever is on the grill.
Useful Ordering Words
大盛りOhmori — large portion

Tsukiji Outer Market — How It Works
Walk the market first — check all stalls before buying. Most stalls serve standing. Point at what you want. Hand over cash. No need to speak. Budget ¥2,500 total. Best stalls: Daiwazushi (tuna), Shimizu (matcha), Tsukiji Takewaka (tamagoyaki).
Konbini (Convenience Store) — Your Best Friend
7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart — open 24hrs, everywhere. Hot food counter, fresh onigiri (¥130–200), sandwiches, ramen cups, beer, umbrellas, phone chargers, ATMs. Don't skip konbini breakfast — quality is genuinely good and ¥400 fills you up.

Speak quietly on trains and in restaurants. Japanese public spaces are noticeably quiet.
🚶
Walk on the left on escalators — stand left, walk right in Tokyo (opposite to Osaka).
💴
Hand cash with both hands or use the small tray on the counter — never just slap money down.
👟
Remove shoes at ryokans and some restaurants with tatami floors — look for a step up at the entrance.
🛁
Wash before the onsen — shower completely before entering any shared bath. This is non-negotiable.
❌ Never Do
🍱
Don't eat while walking — eating standing at a stall is fine, but walking and eating on the street is considered rude.
📱
Don't talk on the phone on trains or buses. Move to the vestibule between carriages if you must take a call.
🪴
Don't tip — tipping is not done in Japan and can actually cause offence. Service is already excellent.
🗑️
Don't litter — there are almost no public bins in Japan yet streets are immaculate. Carry a small bag for rubbish.
👘
Don't wear shoes into a ryokan room — you'll be given slippers. Remove those too before stepping onto tatami.
🎋
Don't skip the queues — Japan queues for everything (taxis, trains, ramen). Always find the end of the line.
🌸 Cherry Blossom Season Specific
🌸
Don't shake the trees — it's extremely frowned upon. Let petals fall naturally.
🧺
Hanami picnic rules — in Ueno Park, blue tarps are laid out by groups. You can sit anywhere not reserved. Alcohol is allowed in Ueno, not in Shinjuku Gyoen.
🌅
Go early for the best experience — Senso-ji and Chidorigafuchi at 06:00–07:00 are extraordinary. By 09:00 the crowds have arrived.

✦ Key Tips

💳 Suica Card

Buy at the airport. Load ¥10,000. Covers metro, JR, buses and konbini across all 7 cities.

💴 Cash

Japan is largely cash-based. Keep ¥10,000 in small bills. 7-Eleven ATMs accept foreign cards everywhere.

🎌 Yukata at Ryokans

Provided at Fukuzumiro and Ryokan Sansui. Left side always over right — right over left is for the deceased.

🚅 Hikari 645 — Mt Fuji

Car 8, Seats 2-C & 2-E. Fuji on the RIGHT after Mishima. Seat 2-E is the window.

🚃 Romancecar — Rebook

Cancel original 13:30 booking. Rebook 10:00 departure Mar 28 at odakyu.jp. Arrive Hakone-Yumoto 11:30 — full afternoon for the Hakone Loop.

🚂 Hida Express — Gorge Views

Platform 11, Nagoya. LEFT seats after Gifu for gorge. Seats physically rotate at Gifu — turn yours before moving.

💊 Medication Storage

Ozempic/Mounjaro: carry-on only, 2–8°C. Email all 7 hotels to arrange fridge storage before arrival.

📦 Takkyubin Luggage

Ship bags hotel-to-hotel. ¥2,000–2,500/bag. Ask Metropolitan Edmont to send to Keio Prelia Kyoto on Mar 27 evening.

🛁 Onsen Rules

Always wash completely before entering shared baths. Tattoos may bar entry to some onsens — private baths at both ryokans bypass this.

🍣 Never Double Dip

Kushikatsu in Osaka has communal dipping sauce. Double dipping is the cardinal rule violation — use the cabbage to add more sauce instead.

🏯 Kyoto Bus Pass

¥700 day pass if using buses 3+ times. Buy on the bus or at Kyoto Station. Covers all city buses — essential for temple-hopping days.
